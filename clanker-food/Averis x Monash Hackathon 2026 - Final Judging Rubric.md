**Averis x Monash Hackathon 2026 — Final Round Judging Criteria**

*Use this page as a quick reference. The full rubric follows on the next page.*

**Scoring overview**

**100 points total: Technical 70 points • Product & Impact 30 points**

The largest single criterion is End-to-End Functionality at 25 points.


**Criteria at a glance**

Technical (70): End-to-End Functionality 25 | Architecture & Scalability 15 | Technology Integration 15 | Engineering Quality & Robustness 15

Product & Impact (30): Solution Effectiveness & User Value 10 | User Experience & Differentiation 10 | Impact & Future Potential 10

**How to mark**

• Score all seven criteria using whole numbers.

• Use the performance bands as guidance and score each criterion independently.

• Do not reward the same evidence twice.

• Base scores on what is demonstrated, submitted or clearly explained.

**TECHNICAL — 70 POINTS**

| No. | Criterion | Max | What judges assess | Weak | Developing | Strong | Excellent |
| :---: | :---- | :---: | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | End-to-End Functionality | 25 | How completely and reliably the finished solution works across its main user and system flows. | Major flows are broken, missing or depend heavily on mock-ups or manual substitution. | Core features work, but several flows are incomplete, fragile or require workarounds. | Main flows work end-to-end with only minor issues. | The finished solution works reliably end-to-end with major features and integrations functioning as intended. |
| 2 | Architecture & Scalability | 15 | How well the system is designed, including component structure, data flow, dependencies and ability to grow. | Architecture is unclear or poorly suited to the solution. | The design is workable, but key decisions or scaling needs are unclear. | Clear architecture with sensible component separation and scaling decisions. | Well-justified architecture with clear trade-offs and a realistic scaling approach, supported by the implementation or other technical evidence. |
| 3 | Technology Integration | 15 | How appropriately and effectively the chosen technologies, tools, libraries, and APIs are integrated to solve the problem, with no restrictions on tech stack. | Technology choices are poorly justified or mismatched for the problem; integration is superficial, broken, or primarily cosmetic.  | Technologies are functional within the project, but the integration is basic, relies heavily on boilerplate code, or shows limited technical depth. | Technologies are well-chosen and effectively integrated; components communicate cleanly and meaningfully contribute to the core solution. | Deep, seamless integration of modern or complex technologies; tools are leveraged to their full potential with strong technical craftsmanship.  |
| 4 | Engineering Quality & Robustness | 15 | Quality and reliability of the implementation, including testing, error handling, security and performance where relevant. | The implementation is fragile, with little evidence of testing or error handling. | Basic safeguards are present, but reliability or testing is limited. | The implementation is tested and handles common errors reliably. Security and performance are considered where relevant. | The implementation is well tested, reliable and handles failures appropriately, showing strong engineering discipline. |

**PRODUCT & IMPACT — 30 POINTS**

| No. | Criterion | Max | What judges assess | Weak | Developing | Strong | Excellent |
| :---: | :---- | :---: | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 5 | Solution Effectiveness & User Value | 10 | How well the finished solution addresses the problem statement and delivers useful value to its intended users or stakeholders. | The solution does not clearly address the problem or deliver useful value. | The solution addresses part of the problem, but value is limited or unclear. | The solution clearly addresses the problem and provides meaningful value. | Strong problem-solution fit with clear evidence that the finished solution provides meaningful value. |
| 6 | User Experience & Differentiation | 10 | How easy the solution is to use and how meaningfully it differs from common alternatives. Consider both equally. | The experience is confusing and the solution shows little differentiation. | The solution is usable, but either the experience or differentiation is limited. | The main experience is clear and intuitive, with meaningful differentiation. | The solution is polished, intuitive and clearly differentiated in a way that improves the user experience. |
| 7 | Impact & Future Potential | 10 | Potential for measurable impact, adoption and continued development beyond the hackathon. | There is little credible impact or path beyond the hackathon. | Some potential is visible, but assumptions or next steps are vague. | Clear impact potential with realistic next steps and useful measures of success. | Strong impact potential with a credible adoption or growth path and clear measures of success. |

Scoring note: use the performance bands as guidance and award a whole-number score within the relevant range. Score each criterion independently and avoid rewarding the same evidence twice.
